Bio


When I was no more than 2-3 months old, my two-year-old sister, Merry Elliot, M.E., drowned in my grandmother's home pool. My parents were simply in another room for no longer than ten minutes. Her and I were in the playroom. Our older sister was not home. M.E. opened the back door and jumped into the pool without floaties for the first time.


I was a particularly happy baby. I'm told that I almost never cried. I’m told without the brightness that I created with my thoughtless, infant smiles and laughter; the rest of my family would have drowned in the heavy, dark, shadow of grief that seeped and festered into my family's life, home, and hearts.


Ever since I was 4, the age my second, older sister was when M.E. died, I’ve lived with these stories of intense emotion and powerful truths that I was a part of but can only imagine.


Yet I grieve and cry. I’m crying right now, over a girl who held and loved me, but that I cannot remember.


If my parents never told me her stories, I wouldn't feel such emotion. But if I can never know her love, then the sadness of her loss will have to do.


And if stories are so powerful that they can hold, preserve, and pass on something as special as the love I feel from her now, there must be no limit to what they can do.


I try to give people a chance to love, or mourn, or admire, or hate, or just to feel something from the worlds I create.


And there is no good or bad feeling. Because feeling something is always better than feeling nothing.
